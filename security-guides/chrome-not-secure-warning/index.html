What Does “Not Secure” Mean in Chrome?

Understand Chrome's Not secure warning, HTTPS, certificate errors and what information you should avoid entering on an unsafe connection.

What the warning usually means

“Not secure” commonly means the page is using an unencrypted HTTP connection or has another connection-security problem. Information sent through an unencrypted connection may be exposed or altered while travelling between the browser and the website.

What it does not prove

The warning does not automatically prove that the website contains malware. Similarly, a padlock or HTTPS connection does not prove that the website is honest. HTTPS confirms an encrypted connection to a particular domain, so you must still check that the domain is the one you intended to visit.

What you should avoid entering

  • Passwords or recovery codes
  • Payment or banking information
  • Private messages or identifying details
  • Files that contain confidential information

When to leave immediately

Leave if Chrome displays a full-page certificate warning, the domain is unexpected, the page requests sensitive information, or the site asks you to install an unfamiliar certificate, profile, extension or program.

Safer next steps

  1. Check the complete domain in the address bar.
  2. Open the service through its official app or a known bookmark.
  3. Do not bypass certificate warnings on important accounts.
  4. Update the browser and confirm the device's date and time.
  5. Use the link checker to review visible URL warning signs.
