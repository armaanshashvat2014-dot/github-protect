10 Signs a Download Website Is Fake

Learn ten practical warning signs of fake software download pages, misleading installers and impersonated publisher websites.

Fake download pages often copy logos and screenshots from real software. Judge the complete source and behavior of the page—not its appearance alone.

Ten warning signs

  1. The domain is slightly misspelled. Added words, swapped letters and unfamiliar endings can imitate a real publisher.
  2. An advertisement looks like the main download button. Ads may lead to unrelated installers.
  3. The page offers software the publisher does not provide. Verify the product on the official company website.
  4. The browser starts a download without a clear click. Cancel unexpected files and review the Downloads list.
  5. The site demands that protection be disabled. Do not turn off security controls to make an installer run.
  6. The filename is misleading. Double extensions and names imitating documents or updates are warning signs.
  7. Publisher information is missing or inconsistent. Compare the file signature, company name and official product page.
  8. The page creates extreme urgency. Fake countdowns and claims that the device is already infected are designed to rush decisions.
  9. The connection or certificate produces a browser warning. Do not enter information or continue past certificate errors.
  10. Independent details do not match. Version numbers, release dates, file sizes or hashes differ from the publisher's information.

A safer download method

Search results and advertisements are starting points, not proof of identity. Open the publisher's confirmed domain, navigate to its download section, confirm the product and scan the downloaded file before opening it.

HTTPS protects a connection to a domain; it does not prove that the domain belongs to the company you intended to visit.
